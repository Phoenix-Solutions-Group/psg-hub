## Executive Summary

This document is the CMO execution plan for the Body Shop Marketer (BSM) launch. It translates the strategic roadmap approved in PSG-109 into an actionable go-to-market playbook across six areas: brand positioning, content strategy, demand generation, beta recruitment, trade show/partnership outreach, and validation methodology.

BSM enters a market where 55%+ of collision repair shops spend less than $5,000/year on marketing — not because they don't want leads, but because no platform has ever spoken their language or proven ROI in their terms. Our edge is 35 years of collision repair domain expertise embedded in a product that costs less than one repair order per month.

The marketing strategy is built around a single insight: **shop owners buy trust, not technology.** Every channel, campaign, and message is designed to earn trust before asking for a sale.

## 1. Brand Positioning Brief

### 1.1 Target Audience

**Primary Buyer: The Independent Shop Owner-Operator**

| Attribute | Profile |
|-----------|---------|
| Title | Owner, General Manager, or Owner-Manager |
| Shop size | 3-15 bays, 1-2 locations, $750K-$3M annual revenue |
| Age | 42-58 |
| Marketing spend | <$5K/year (often zero or word-of-mouth only) |
| Tech comfort | Low; uses CCC or Mitchell for ops; avoids unfamiliar software |
| Pain points | Not enough claimants, heavy insurance DRP dependence, poor online reviews, no time |
| Motivators | More ROs, CSI scores, referrals, family legacy, beating the shop down the street |
| Decision style | Skeptical of pitches; trusts peers and industry associations; wants proof before paying |
| Buying trigger | Lost a DRP, had a bad agency experience, heard about a competitor winning on Google |

**Secondary Buyer: MSO Operations Manager (3-10 locations)**
Oversees marketing across multiple locations, currently managing inconsistent brand presence and no centralized reporting. Evaluates tools on scalability, not price sensitivity. Enters through Performance or Multi-Location tier.

**Tertiary Persona: The Ambitious Single-Shop Owner** (Growth tier)
Technically comfortable enough to engage with a dashboard. Actively searching for ways to grow volume without adding headcount. Age 32-45. More receptive to self-serve onboarding and ROI calculators.

### 1.2 Core Value Proposition

> **Body Shop Marketer is the only AI-powered marketing platform built exclusively for collision repair shops, replacing $2,000-$5,000/month agency retainers with automated, data-driven marketing that understands your customers, your market, and your industry.**

This proposition rests on three legs:
1. **Vertical specialization** — Built for collision repair, not generic auto, not general small business.
2. **Cost displacement** — BSM isn't a new expense; it replaces a more expensive one. At $199-$999/mo, 60-80% cheaper than agency alternatives.
3. **Automated results** — Shop owners get leads, reviews, and content without spending hours on marketing.

### 1.3 Competitive Differentiation

| Competitor Category | Their Gap | Our Wedge |
|--------------------|-----------|-----------|
| Marketing agencies | Expensive, slow, no transparency, generic output | 60-80% lower cost; 24/7 AI generation; full attribution dashboard; collision-specific output |
| Horizontal reputation tools (Podium, Birdeye) | Broad automotive, no collision expertise | 8 collision-specific customer personas; claimant capture funnels; insurance-aware messaging |
| Shop management add-ons (CCC, Tekmetric) | Marketing is an afterthought | Full-funnel coverage; LocalReach SEO engine; Google Ads management; email/SMS automation |
| DIY | Owner time is worth $150-$200/hr; inconsistent results | Saves 10-15 hrs/month; professional quality; proven frameworks |

### 1.4 Messaging Framework

**Headline:** *Marketing that speaks collision repair. Starting at $199/month.*

**Tagline options (3 for board selection):**
1. *You fix cars. We fix your marketing.*
2. *More claimants. Better reviews. Less work.*
3. *The only marketing platform that knows your industry.*

**Message architecture by funnel stage:**

| Stage | Core Message | Proof Point |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Awareness | "Most shops are invisible online, and losing claimants because of it." | Stat: 55% of shops spend <$5K/yr on marketing |

### 1.5 Brand Voice
Confident, plain-spoken, outcomes-anchored. Uses shop-specific language (ROs, claimants, DRP, CSI), real numbers, peer proof. Avoids jargon and generic digital marketing language.

### 2.2 ARPU Reconciliation

PSG-109 contains an internal discrepancy: $526/mo in unit economics vs. $599/mo in the SOM section. Calculated from the stated tier mix (30/45/20/5):

| Tier | Price | Share | Contribution |
|------|-------|-------|-------------|
| Essentials | $199 | 30% | $59.70 |
| Growth | $499 | 45% | $224.55 |
| Performance | $999 | 20% | $199.80 |
| Multi-Location | $1,499 | 5% | $74.95 |
| **List price ARPU** | | **100%** | **$559/mo** |

The $526/mo figure is internally consistent when adjusted for a 15% annual plan discount applied to 40% of customers:

> 60% monthly x $559 + 40% annual x ($559 x 0.85) = $335 + $190 = **$525/mo, approximately $526/mo** (verified)

This means $526/mo **implicitly assumes** 40% of customers elect annual billing. The $599/mo SOM figure appears to be the Growth tier anchor rounded up and is not a calculated blended average. Do not use $599 for financial projections.

**This model uses $526/mo as base ARPU throughout. Sensitivity analysis uses $480/mo (bear) and $570/mo (bull).**

## 1. Technical Architecture

### 1.1 System Overview

BSM is a multi-tenant SaaS platform with five major subsystems:

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        CLIENT LAYER                             |
|   Next.js 14 (App Router) -- Vercel Edge -- shop.bsm.psgweb.me |
+-----------------------------+----------------------------------+
                              | HTTPS / Server Actions
+-----------------------------v----------------------------------+
|                       API LAYER                                 |
|   Next.js API Routes + tRPC -- type-safe, edge-ready            |
|   Auth: Supabase Auth (JWT, RLS enforced at DB level)           |
+-------+----------+----------+-----------+----------------------+
        |          |          |           |
+-------v-+ +-----v---+ +---v------+ +--v---------------------+
|Supabase | |BigQuery  | |AI Worker | |Integration Hub         |
|Postgres | |(analytics| |(Claude)  | |(Google/SendGrid/Twilio)|
|+ Storage| |pipeline) |          | |                        |
+---------+ +---------+ +----------+ +------------------------+
```

### 1.2 Frontend

| Concern | Choice | Rationale |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| Framework | Next.js 14 (App Router) | SSR for SEO on public-facing LocalReach pages; RSC reduces client bundle |
| Language | TypeScript | Type safety across full stack |
| Styling | Tailwind CSS + shadcn/ui | Fast iteration; consistent component library |
| State | Zustand (client), React Query (server state) | Minimal boilerplate; cache-aware data fetching |
| Forms | react-hook-form + zod | Validation shared with backend schemas |
| Charts | Recharts | Lightweight; sufficient for analytics dashboard |

**Public routes** (shop subdomain or slug): LocalReach pages, review landing pages — statically generated at build, ISR for updates.

**App routes** (authenticated): dashboard, content calendar, reputation inbox, ads manager, settings.

### 1.3 Backend / API

- **tRPC** over Next.js API routes — end-to-end typesafe, no separate REST spec to maintain
- **Supabase Auth** for session management; JWT validated at middleware layer
- **Row-Level Security (RLS)** enforced at Postgres — every query scoped to `shop_id` automatically
- **Background jobs**: Supabase Edge Functions (lightweight triggers) + BullMQ on Railway for heavy async work (AI generation, bulk email dispatch, analytics sync)

### 1.4 Database (Supabase / PostgreSQL)

**Core schema tables:**
```
shops              -- tenant root; one row per shop
shop_users         -- junction: user <-> shop (role: owner | manager | viewer)
locations          -- for multi-location MSOs
content_items      -- AI-generated posts, pages, emails
review_items       -- ingested reviews from Google/Yelp/etc.
review_responses   -- AI-drafted responses
campaigns          -- email/SMS campaign definitions
campaign_sends     -- individual send log
leads              -- captured from forms / call tracking
analytics_snapshots-- denormalized daily rollups from BQ
integrations       -- per-shop credential vault (encrypted)
ai_prompt_templates-- versioned prompt library
ai_jobs            -- generation queue; status tracking
```

**Multi-tenancy:** `shop_id UUID NOT NULL` on every tenant-scoped table + RLS policies:
```
CREATE POLICY shop_isolation ON content_items
  USING (shop_id = auth.jwt() -> 'shop_id');
```

**Credential vault:** Integration secrets stored encrypted in Supabase Vault; never in plain columns.

### 1.5 AI Content Generation Pipeline

```
Trigger (schedule / event / manual)
    |
    v
Job enqueued in ai_jobs (BullMQ)
    |
    v
Worker: fetch shop profile + persona config + prompt template
    |
    v
Claude API call (claude-sonnet-4-6)
  - system: collision repair persona + brand voice + compliance rules
  - user: specific content request (type, topic, target keyword, length)
    |
    v
Quality validation layer
  - terminology checker (regex + embeddings vs. approved glossary)
  - brand voice scorer (cosine similarity vs. shop samples)
  - compliance check (no pricing claims, no guaranteed results language)
    |
    v
If validation passes: content_items (status: pending_review)
If fails: retry with refined prompt (max 2 retries) then human_review queue
    |
    v
CSM review interface: approve / edit / reject
    |
    v
Published (scheduled or immediate)
```

**Model selection rationale:**
- `claude-sonnet-4-6`: primary content generation — best quality/cost ratio for 500-1000 token outputs
- `claude-haiku-4-5`: review response drafts, short social posts — lower latency/cost acceptable
- Embeddings: `text-embedding-3-small` (OpenAI) for terminology similarity checks

**Prompt architecture:**
- Base system prompt: shared collision repair domain knowledge
- Shop persona layer: injected per-shop (brand voice, services, location, certifications)
- Customer persona layer: one of 8 PSG personas matched to content type
- Content directive: specific task with SEO target, format, word count

**Cost model:** ~$0.003/content piece at sonnet pricing. At 30 pieces/mo for Performance tier: $0.09/shop/mo in AI compute.

## 1. Sales Playbook

### 1.1 Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) by Tier

**Essentials Tier ICP ($199/mo)**
- Shop type: Independent single-location shop, <$1.5M annual revenue
- Marketing status: No formal marketing; owner relies on word-of-mouth and insurance DRPs
- Digital presence: Google Business Profile claimed but unoptimized; few reviews (<25)
- Pain point: "I don't have time to market. I'm in the shop every day."
- Decision maker: Owner-operator. Often the technician who became the owner.
- Trigger: Lost a customer to a competitor because they couldn't be found online; or got stung by a bad review they ignored
- Disqualifiers: No Google Business Profile; shop closure risk; owner resistant to any digital tool

**Growth Tier ICP ($499/mo)**
- Shop type: Independent shop, $1.5M-$4M annual revenue, 4-15 employees
- Marketing status: Either no marketing or currently paying an agency/Podium $500-$1,500/mo and underwhelmed by results
- Digital presence: Has a website (often generic), some Google reviews (25-80), sporadic social posts
- Pain point: "My marketing feels like a black box. I don't know if it's working."
- Decision maker: Owner or GM; may involve spouse/office manager
- Trigger: Recent shop visit from a competitor rep; read about a peer shop getting leads from SEO; unhappy with agency response time
- Disqualifiers: Already on a long-term agency contract; owner is anti-digital; shop undergoing sale/transition

**Performance Tier ICP ($999/mo)**
- Shop type: High-volume shop $4M-$8M annual revenue OR 2-location operation
- Marketing status: Currently paying agency $1,500-$3,000/mo; wants more control and transparency
- Digital presence: Strong (100+ reviews, active social), but disjointed across platforms
- Pain point: "I pay my agency $2,500/month and I don't know what they actually do."
- Decision maker: Owner or operations manager; sometimes has a part-time marketing coordinator
- Trigger: Agency missed a deadline; couldn't get accurate attribution; read a case study about BSM ROI
- Disqualifiers: Already has in-house marketing staff; part of a large MSO with corporate marketing

**Multi-Location Tier ICP ($1,499+/mo)**
- Shop type: MSO with 3-10 locations; $8M+ combined revenue
- Marketing status: Inconsistent marketing across locations; each GM does their own thing
- Pain point: "Every shop has a different look, different reviews, different marketing. It's a mess."
- Decision maker: COO or VP Operations; CEO approval required
- Trigger: Preparing for franchise model; recent acquisition; investor asking about marketing standardization
- Disqualifiers: 10+ locations with dedicated in-house team; PE-backed with agency relationship locked by PE sponsor

### 1.2 Discovery Call Script and Qualification Framework

**Pre-Call Research Checklist (5 min before every call)**
- Google Business Profile: claimed? rating? review count? response rate?
- Website: exists? mobile-friendly? any SEO content?
- Current marketing vendor (check their site footer/social handles)
- Shop size (IBISWorld, Google Maps reviews volume as proxy)
- DRP affiliations (check State Farm, Allstate, USAA logos on site)
- LinkedIn: owner profile? How long in business?

**Discovery Call Script (30 minutes)**

**Opening (2 min):** "[Name], thanks for making time. I'll be direct — I have about six questions I want to ask you, and then I want to show you something specific to your shop. Sound good?"

**Situation Questions (8 min):**
- "Walk me through how you're currently getting new customers — what's working, what's not?"
- "What's your Google rating right now? About how many reviews do you have?"
- "Are you on any DRP programs? How much of your volume is insurance work vs. customer-pay?"
- "Are you spending anything on marketing right now — agency, ads, anything?"

**Problem/Impact Questions (8 min):**
- "When a claimant has a choice, what's your best guess on why they pick your shop vs. a competitor down the street?"
- "Has there been a time you felt like you lost business to a shop that wasn't actually better than you, just better at marketing?"
- "What happens when someone leaves you a bad review — do you respond? Does anyone on your team handle that?"

**Need/Payoff Questions (5 min):**
- "If you could fix one thing about how customers find and choose your shop, what would it be?"
- "What would 10 additional repair orders per month mean to your business? [Pause for their number.] That's about $[X] in additional revenue."

**Qualification Gate: BANT adapted for SMB (3 min):**
- Budget: "We work with shops at a few different investment levels — starting at $199/month. Is that a conversation worth having?"
- Authority: "When you decide to invest in something like this, is that a decision you make, or does anyone else weigh in?"
- Need: (Already confirmed above)
- Timeline: "Are you looking to make a change in the next 30 days, or more like later this year?"

**Transition to Demo (2 min):** "Here's what I'd like to do — let me pull up what Body Shop Marketer would look like specifically for [shop name] and your market. It takes about 15 minutes. Are you at a computer right now?"

**Disqualification criteria — exit politely if:**
- Shop has fewer than 10 cars/week (ROI not compelling)
- Owner says "I'm selling in the next 6 months"
- Owner is hostile to digital marketing on principle
- No decision authority and no path to the decision maker

### 1.3 Demo Flow and Talk Track

**Demo Principles**
- Always demo their market, not a generic shop. Use their shop name, zip code, and one real competitor.
- Lead with outcomes, not features. Never say "and here's the dashboard" — say "here's how you'll see your leads."

**Demo Flow (20 minutes)**

**Act 1: The Problem (3 min)** — Screen share their GBP, current review count vs. competitor. "When a claimant searches 'collision repair near me', here's what they see."

**Act 3: ROI and Close (5 min):** "If BSM generates just 8 additional repair orders per month at your average RO value of $[X], that's $[Y]. At $499/month, you're getting $[Z]:1 ROI."

- Bidding: Maximize Conversions (learning phase); Target CPA once 50+ conversions accumulated
- Budget: 30-40% of total monthly ad spend
- Conversion tracking: Calls + form fills + directions

## 6. Automated Bid Strategy Recommendations

### 6.1 Bid Strategy by Campaign Phase

| Phase | Duration | Strategy | Trigger to Advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch | Days 1-30 | Maximize Clicks (branded); Maximize Conversions (all others) | 30+ conversions in campaign |
| Learning | Days 31-60 | Maximize Conversions | 50+ conversions; stable CVR |
| Optimization | Day 61+ | Target CPA (non-branded, service); Target ROAS (branded) | 90+ days data; consistent CPA |
| Scale | Ongoing | Target ROAS | Proven CPA within range; budget increase approved |

### 6.2 Target CPA Settings at Optimization Phase
Set Target CPA at 110% of actual CPA from the learning phase. Do not set a CPA target lower than observed CPA until 90+ days of data.

### 6.3 Bidding Rules
- Do not set Target CPA below $25 for non-branded. Google will reduce volume to meet an unrealistically low target.
- Do not switch from Maximize Conversions to Target CPA before 50 conversions. The algorithm needs data.
- Do not use Target Impression Share on non-branded campaigns. Reserve for branded only.

### 6.4 Enhanced CPC Note
Do not use Enhanced CPC. It is a legacy strategy. Smart Bidding (Maximize Conversions, Target CPA, Target ROAS) outperforms it in all collision repair accounts with sufficient conversion data.

### 6.5 Geographic Bid Adjustments

| Location Tier | Bid Adjustment | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Within 5 miles of shop | +25% | Highest conversion probability |
| 5-10 miles | +10% | Core service radius |
| 10-20 miles | 0% | Standard |
| 20-30 miles | -15% | Lower conversion rate; maintain presence |
| 30+ miles | -30% or excluded | Diminishing returns unless rural market |

Rural markets: extend radii (20-30 miles may be realistic).
Multi-location: use location-specific radius targeting; bid toward nearest location.

### 6.6 Audience Layering

Apply as observation initially; bid up on confirmed high-value segments after 45 days.

| Audience | Bid Modifier | Source |

Exclude past converters from non-branded campaigns after 90 days to reduce waste on existing customers.

## 8. Quality Score Management

Quality Score is a system, not a standalone metric. Manage all three components together.

| Component | Target | How to Maintain |
|---|---|---|
| Expected CTR | Above average | Test headlines continuously; pause underperformers at 30-day mark |
| Ad relevance | Above average | One theme per ad group; match headline language to keyword |
| Landing page experience | Above average | Page speed 75+; copy mirrors ad; single clear CTA |

Ad Rank formula: Bid x Quality Score x Expected Impact of Extensions. A shop with a $4 bid and Quality Score 8 outranks a competitor with a $6 bid and Quality Score 4. Invest in landing page quality before increasing bids. That is the highest-leverage optimization available.
